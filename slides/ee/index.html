Three areas · Research + Teaching
01 · Predictive Analytics
New tools for predicting who will buy, when, and why
Purchase histories hold enormous predictive signal. The challenge is building models flexible enough to extract it — and general enough to transfer across settings.
References
Dew et al. (2024), "Probabilistic Machine Learning: New Frontiers for Modeling Consumers and their Choices," International Journal of Research in Marketing
Dew, Ascarza, Netzer, and Sicherman (2023), "Detecting Routines: Applications to Ridesharing Customer Relationship Management," Journal of Marketing Research, 61(2), 368–392
Dew and Ansari (2018), "Bayesian Nonparametric Customer Base Analysis with Model-based Visualizations," Marketing Science, 37(2), 216–235
01
Classic data, new methods — and better predictions
  • Detecting customer routines: new machine learning models distinguish routine from non-routine purchasing, sharpening predictions and revealing hidden segments.
  • Heterogeneity is everywhere: customers differ in ways that matter. Flexible machine learning models that capture this variation substantially outperform standard approaches.
  • Real-world deployment: methods applied with ride-sharing companies, mobile gaming platforms, and retail partners.
01 · Predictive Analytics · Teaching and Exec Ed
Bringing research to class — topics I love teaching
  • Customer Lifetime Value: what it is, why it matters, and how to build strategies around growing it
  • Managing churn: prediction, intervention timing, and the economics of retention
  • Heterogeneity and STP: connecting statistical customer variation to classic segmentation, targeting, and positioning frameworks
  • ML-driven personalization: from segmentation to individual-level predictions — building practical, defensible models

02 · Gen AI and Unstructured Data
Making creative and research processes data-driven
At the time of our logo paper, people said: "there's no way we can model something as creative as design." We proved them wrong — and opened a new research agenda.
References
Dew (2024), "Adaptive Preference Measurement with Unstructured Data," Management Science, 71(5), 3996–4012
He and Dew (2026), "Optimal Product Design Synthesis: Pairing Generative Models with Adaptive Preference Measurement," Working Paper
Dew, Ansari, and Toubia (2022), "Letting Logos Speak: Leveraging Multiview Representation Learning for Data-Driven Logo Design," Marketing Science, 41(2), 401–425
AI-Driven Conjoint Analysis (Work in Progress, with Lan E. Luo)
02
Generative AI as a marketing research tool
  • Data-driven branding and logo design: the first marketing paper to treat creative design as an analytical problem — using generative AI to turn subjective aesthetic decisions into a structured, data-driven process.
  • Adaptive preference measurement: consumers respond to visuals, not just words. Generative image models learn individual preferences rapidly from a small number of interactions.
  • AI-driven conjoint analysis (work in progress): we use AI-enabled survey technology to explore exactly what influences consumer choice, modernizing traditional survey methods. The goal is to understand what actually drives market shares: the product, or how it's framed?
02 · Unstructured Data · Influencers
Who should brands work with, and how should influencers manage their audiences?
Answering these questions requires analyzing content at scale: text, images, and engagement patterns that reveal what actually drives influence and brand lift.
References
Tian, Dew, and Iyengar (2024), "Mega or Micro? Influencer Selection Using Follower Elasticity," Journal of Marketing Research, 61(3), 472–495
Tian, Dew, and Iyengar (2026), "How Do Influencers Learn From Feedback?" Working Paper
02
Not all influence is created equal
  • Mega vs. micro influencers: a large following isn't always better. We quantify how reach, engagement, and cost effectiveness vary systematically with influencer size in ways brands often get wrong.
  • Strategic follower management: ongoing work studying the decisions influencers face — what to post, how to grow, and how to sustain authentic relationships over time.
  • The unstructured data angle: content-level analysis — images, text, tone — reveals what actually drives engagement, brand affinity, and purchase intent.
02 · Generative AI · Teaching and Exec Ed
Cutting through the hype — and knowing when AI works
  • Synthetic respondents and digital twins: enormous promise in marketing research, but also real risk — I help students understand when these ideas work, and when they don't
  • Product design and conjoint analysis: quantifying trade-offs, designing good studies, and connecting consumer preferences to product strategy
  • The science of visual design: what data tell us about branding, aesthetics, and creative decisions, and how AI is changing each

03 · Digital Marketing Measurement
Privacy is disrupting everything — and forcing better science
Cookie deprecation and privacy regulation are pushing firms back to marketing mix models. My research asks: when do these models work, and when do they mislead?
References
Dew, Padilla, and Shchetkina (2025), "Your MMM is Broken," Working Paper
Padilla, Dew, and Campbell (2026), "Unified Marketing Measurement: How to Fuse Experimental and Marketing Mix Data," Working Paper
Dew, Ansari, and Li (2020), "Modeling Dynamic Heterogeneity Using Gaussian Processes," Journal of Marketing Research, 57(1), 55–77
Dynamic Marketing Mix Models (Work in Progress, with Shchetkina and Padilla)
03
Building and breaking marketing mix models
  • Marketing mix models (MMMs) are making a comeback: but can the data support the complex models practitioners are fitting? We urge caution — key effects like diminishing returns and time-varying effectiveness are often conflated.
  • The best solution? Combining MMMs with experiments — and we show exactly how to do this properly.
  • Strategic experimentation (in progress): experiments are necessary but expensive. How do we design them well, run them at the right time, and measure true incrementality?
  • Dynamic consumer sensitivities: price elasticities and ad responsiveness change over time — ignoring this leads to systematically wrong conclusions. I've built models of this for both individual consumer and marketing mix data.
03 · Digital Measurement · Teaching and Exec Ed
Why experimentation should be central to every marketing org
  • Digital marketing fundamentals: ad types, metrics, auctions, cookies, and attribution — what every marketer should understand about how the digital advertising ecosystem works
  • The challenge of measurement: why digital marketing is so hard to measure effectively — signal-to-noise issues, selection bias, and why most attribution models give you the wrong answer
  • The case for experimentation: how badly things go without experiments, and how to make testing a cultural and organizational priority
  • Marketing Mix Models: what they can and can't tell you, how to build them effectively, and how to avoid the most common misinterpretations in practice
