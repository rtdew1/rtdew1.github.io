UMM · Background

Marketing mix models

  • Very long history in marketing
    Borden (1964), Palda (1965), Bultez & Naert (1979), Little (1979), Winer (1979), …, Hanssens et al. (2003), …
  • At its heart: just regression
    $$y_t = \sum_j \beta_j\, x_{jt} + \epsilon_t \quad\text{— or —}\quad \log y_t = \sum_j \beta_j \log x_{jt} + \epsilon_t$$
  • …with many well-documented issues and extensions
    • Long-term effects, often captured through AdStock: \(S(x_t; L, \alpha) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} \alpha^{\ell} x_{t-\ell} \,\big/ \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} \alpha^{\ell}\)
    • Nonlinear effects: \(y_t = \sum_j f_j(x_{jt}) + \epsilon_t\)
    • Time-varying effects: \(y_t = \sum_j \beta_{jt}\, x_{jt} + \epsilon_t\)

UMM · Model

Unified marketing measurement

$$\log y_t \;=\; \beta_t \log x_t \;+\; \xi_t, \qquad \operatorname{corr}(\log x_t,\, \xi_t) \;=\; \rho_t$$
  • \(\beta_t\): time-varying effectiveness, the elasticity of revenue to spend
  • \(\xi_t\): demand shocks we don't observe
  • \(\rho_t\): time-varying endogeneity, how strongly spend chases the shocks
  • To model dynamics, we treat \(\beta(t)\) and \(\rho(t)\) as functions of time, and model them with Gaussian processes
    • Bayesian way of modeling unknown functions
    • assumes smoothness over time
    • easy to use and estimate, but not strictly necessary (can use any time series model)
UMM · Model

Experiments as measurements

$$b_\tau \;=\; g(\beta_\tau) \;+\; \varepsilon_\tau, \qquad \tau \in \mathcal{T}$$
  • \(b_\tau\): the lift estimates the firm already has, at the times it happened to test
  • \(g(\cdot)\) is the bridge: geo readouts (overlapping window averages), RCT lifts (spend → exposure delivery), on/off flighting (IV)
  • Experiments anchor \(\beta\) locally; the MMM time series shapes it between anchors
Assumption: the experiment and the MMM measure the same underlying effectiveness.
UMM · Results

With static endogeneity, one experiment is enough

  • MMM alone: the shape of \(\beta(t)\) is learnable; the level is not
  • One anchor pins the level; constant \(\rho\) propagates it across the horizon  ⇒  identification is a one-shot problem
UMM · Results

Dynamic endogeneity: one test is not enough

  • Why would \(\rho\) drift? Markets shift, budget rules adapt, and algorithmic buying chases converters: endogeneity by design
  • One anchor is local: confounding drift masquerades as effectiveness drift
  • Tests spread over time disentangle the two paths
Implication: posterior uncertainty tells you when to run the next experiment.
Discussion

Are experiments and MMMs even measuring the same thing?

We assumed yes. Practice often talks as if no.

The folk view vs. our assumption
The folk view
Experiments & attribution → performance: bottom-funnel, short-run, in-market shoppers
MMM → top-funnel: long-run, “future demand”
“They measure different things”
If so, calibrating one with the other is incoherent.
Our assumption
One incrementality, seen through different instruments
Differences are lag, outcome, population, scale: exactly what \(g(\cdot)\) encodes
We assume convertibility; we do not test it
If so, the folk split is a statement about bias and power, not two truths.

Is information acquisition being downweighted?

The purchase funnel

→
Considerationcontent, search, reviews
→
Purchaseretargeting, promos, checkout
  • Top of funnel = awareness = information acquisition
  • It's where both instruments are weakest: effects are slow, small per period, equilibrium-like
    • brands that go dark hold roughly steady for 1–2 years, then erode; a one-quarter test reads “no effect”
  • A good example: brand advertising
    • CMOs prioritize performance over brand roughly 2.5:1 (Gartner 2025)
    • yet 85% agree brand investment drives results
  • What's easy to measure may be what gets funded
UMM · Closing

Key takeaways

1
Standard MMM data underdetermine both the model's shape and its causal level; experiments restore identification.
2
UMM: experiments enter the model as measurements of the same time-varying effectiveness, and uncertainty tells you when to test next.
3
Whether one incrementality spans the whole funnel is an open question, and measurement challenges may affect how firms invest in information.
